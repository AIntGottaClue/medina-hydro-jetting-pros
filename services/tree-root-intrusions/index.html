Part 01

Where the roots get in

Roots are drawn to the moisture in a sewer lateral. A loose joint or a small crack is enough of an opening, and over several seasons the growth fills the pipe. Medina's older streets near the square have mature trees and older lateral pipe, so this is a common find on camera.

Clearing the roots does not close the opening. If the camera shows a gap, plan on repair or a regular cleaning schedule.

Part 02

What the jet does

A root-cutting nozzle runs the length of the line and clears the mass back to the pipe wall at a pressure the pipe can take. Debris flushes down the line with the water.

The second camera pass shows the result. If the wall is cracked or offset, you get that footage to keep.

Part 03

County and homeowner lines

Medina County Sanitary Engineers maintain the sewer main and the lateral inside the public right-of-way. The lateral from your house to the main is yours. The county advises that lateral repair or replacement needs a permit from its Permits Division before work begins, so keep footage of any damaged section.

Questions

Tree Root Intrusions: common questions.

Will roots grow back after jetting?

They can. Jetting removes the roots in the pipe, not the opening they used. The camera shows whether the pipe needs repair.

Does a root problem mean my sewer line is broken?

Roots can enter through a gap or a loose joint. Their presence does not show how much damage there is. Camera footage does.

Do I need a permit to repair a lateral?

The county advises a permit from its Permits Division before repair or replacement. Ask the county before any digging.

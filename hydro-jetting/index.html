How it works

Water, doing the work of a blade.

Hydro jetting feeds a high-pressure hose into the line from a cleanout or drain opening. Water leaves the nozzle at pressures a drain cable can't match, cutting through the blockage and scrubbing the pipe wall as the hose advances.

The Pump

A dedicated jetting unit pushes water at up to 4000 PSI, enough force to cut roots and strip hardened grease without touching the pipe itself.

The Nozzle

Rear-facing jets pull the hose down the line while forward ports break up the blockage. The water does the work, wall to wall.

The Proof

A camera runs the line after the jet. A full-diameter clean is not a claim. It is footage you can watch.

The target list

What jetting removes.

  • Grease and sludge. Hardened kitchen grease and years of soap scum, emulsified and flushed out.
  • Tree roots. Root intrusions cut through and scoured off the pipe wall, not just trimmed back.
  • Mineral scale. Hard-water scale and rust buildup that narrows older lines, stripped to the wall.
  • Sand and sediment. Silt and grit settled in low spots, lifted and carried out of the line.

The comparison

Jetting vs. snaking.

Snaking

  • Bores a small hole through the clog
  • Leaves buildup clinging to the walls
  • The line re-narrows as debris collects again

Hydro Jetting

  • Scours the full diameter of the pipe
  • Strips grease, scale, and roots off the wall
  • Result verified on camera before we leave

Hydro Jetting vs Snaking

A cable opens a hole. A jet cleans the pipe. Which one you need depends on what is in the line and how it has been behaving.

Part 01

What a snake does well

A cable bores through a stoppage and often restores flow quickly. For a one-time clog in a single fixture, that can be enough.

It tends to leave the coating on the wall, so the same line may clog again soon.

Part 02

What a jet does well

Water scours the full inside of the pipe and flushes debris away. It suits grease, scale, sludge and roots in a sound line.

It is not right for every pipe. Weak or failing lines may need another approach after the camera pass.

Part 03

How to choose

Repeat clogs, several fixtures slowing together, or a kitchen with heavy grease use point toward jetting. A one-off stoppage may only need a cable.

Tell us the pattern on the form and we will say which fits.

Questions

Hydro Jetting vs Snaking: common questions.

Is jetting always better?

No. A simple stoppage may only need a cable. Jetting is stronger against buildup on the wall.

Can a jet damage a pipe?

It can damage a weak or failing pipe, which is why the camera comes first.

Which should I ask for?

Describe the symptoms and we will recommend one.

Hydro Jetting Along East Washington Street

The National Register of Historic Places lists the H. G. Blake House at 314 East Washington Street as a Greek Revival single dwelling. The Main Street Medina walking tour credits Mayor H. G. Blake with leading the rebuilding of the downtown after the 1870 fire. Houses near the square from that era have had many owners and many changes.

The city's comprehensive plan says its largest residential district, low density residential, is concentrated south and east of downtown, and that small multi-use areas along Washington Street allow older homes to be repurposed as businesses.

A house of that age may combine old pipe with new fixtures. Jetting suits a sound line, and an inspection says whether yours is.

Hydro Jetting Considerations in East Washington Street

A few questions are worth settling before the visit.

Pipe condition

A camera shows whether the line is sound.

  • Request a camera look
  • Share repair notes
  • Plan repair if damage is found

Exterior work

Cleaning inside a pipe does not change the outside of a building, but excavation can. Check with the city before digging on a historic property.

  • Keep cleaning and repair separate
  • Ask the city first
  • Document findings

Public or private

Cleaning a private line cannot repair a public sewer.

  • Ask neighbors about the same backup
  • Note rain timing
  • Report widespread backups to the city

Hydro jetting questions in East Washington Street

Do historic houses need special handling for jetting?

Cleaning inside a pipe is not an exterior change. Digging may be.

Can roots from big shade trees reach my line?

They can enter older joints.

Is hydro jetting safe on older pipe?

On sound pipe it can be. A damaged section should be assessed first.

Will cleaning repair a broken pipe?

No. Repair is separate.

Why does my basement drain back up first?

It sits at the low point of the system.

Could the problem be the public sewer?

If neighbors see the same backup, it may be. If only your property is affected, start with the private line.

What does a camera inspection show?

Pipe material, joints, roots, deposits and damage.

How often should I clean?

It depends on the cause.

Will one cleaning stop the problem for good?

It clears the buildup present at that visit. Whether it returns depends on the cause.

What should I have ready?

Your address, the affected fixtures, when it began and any repair notes.

Hydro Jetting for Public Square Buildings

The Main Street Medina historic district walking tour explains that Elijah Boardman set aside land in 1817 for a county seat around Public Square, that fires struck in 1848 and 1870, and that the 1870 fire pushed the downtown to rebuild in brick. The district was later restored and listed on the National Register, with overlay zoning created in the early 1990s.

The City of Medina's preservation brochure says properties in the Public Square Historic District must receive a Certificate of Appropriateness before any work that may change the exterior of a structure or site. Cleaning inside a drain does not change the exterior, but excavation to reach or repair a line can, so it is worth confirming first.

The National Park Service record for the Medina Public Square Historic District lists Greek Revival, Second Empire and Italianate styles, a reminder of how many eras of building share this square. Each era left its own plumbing.

What Local Property Owners Often Consider

  • Which tenants or floors share the line
  • Whether a grease trap or interceptor serves a kitchen
  • Basement or rear cleanouts, which may be hard to reach
  • Past renovations that added restrooms or kitchens
  • Whether the owner or manager must approve work
  • Whether any excavation could alter the exterior and need city review

Hydro jetting questions in Public Square Historic District

Does the historic district review drain cleaning?

The city reviews exterior changes. Cleaning inside a pipe is not an exterior change, but digging can be.

Can a restaurant line be cleaned after hours?

Ask when you call so access can be arranged.

Is hydro jetting safe on older pipe?

On sound pipe it can be. A damaged section should be assessed first.

Will cleaning repair a broken pipe?

No. Repair is separate.

Why does my basement drain back up first?

It sits at the low point of the system.

Could the problem be the public sewer?

If neighbors see the same backup, it may be. If only your property is affected, start with the private line.

What does a camera inspection show?

Pipe material, joints, roots, deposits and damage.

How often should I clean?

It depends on the cause.

Will one cleaning stop the problem for good?

It clears the buildup present at that visit. Whether it returns depends on the cause.

What should I have ready?

Your address, the affected fixtures, when it began and any repair notes.
